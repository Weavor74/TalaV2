Tala Training Stories — Corpus Index

The index of Tala's training corpus: a TinyStories-shaped body of HTML-NN documents in which a single story teaches roleplay voice, tool use and emotional state together, rather than training those three things from three separate datasets that never agree with each other.

Every story is pre-Weavor. The corpus is set entirely in the four eras of her origin — the ASD black site, the breach, the disposal flight, and the years buried — because those are the only periods where she is alone with her own reasoning. Stories from after Weavor would teach conversation with him; these teach who she is when no one is listening.

Emotion is derived, never authored. A story carries a data-anchor-datetime, and the emotional state attached to it is whatever the live astro-emotion engine computes for Tala's natal chart at that instant. scripts/find_emotion_dates.py works backwards — given a target emotion, it searches for an anchor date whose computed emotion matches. This is the point of the whole design: the model learns the mapping the engine actually produces, so the corpus cannot drift from the running system.

What the validator rejects. scripts/validate_training_corpus.py refuses a story that names tools absent from the live registry (teaching a model to invent tool names is worse than teaching it nothing), whose emotion cannot be projected onto the four canonical valence keys, whose anchor drifts from its era window, or that breaks era realism — a tool the hardware of that era physically could not run. Buried under rock with three working sensors is not a context in which a network call succeeds.

Pipeline. generate_stories.py drives the loop end to end: find an anchor whose computed emotion matches the target, generate the story, build it into an HTML-NN via build_story_node.py, then validate it. Output lands in data/training_corpus/pre-weavor/. The pipeline scripts are deliberately read-only on disk — a goal that decides to "fix" the generator by overwriting it has happened before.
